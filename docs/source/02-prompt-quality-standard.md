# PROFESSIONAL COMMERCIAL PROMPT STANDARD

The final generated video prompt is the core product of JAS Ad Studio.

It must NOT resemble a short generic AI prompt.

It must read like a professional commercial-production brief written by an experienced advertising creative director, cinematographer, motion director and AI video prompt engineer.

The platform must be capable of generating prompts at the depth, specificity and production quality of a world-class product-commercial brief.

The prompt should give the target video generator enough information to understand:

- what the product is;
- what must remain unchanged;
- the advertising concept;
- the emotional tone;
- the visual style;
- the environment;
- product behavior;
- camera behavior;
- lighting behavior;
- scene progression;
- editing rhythm;
- material treatment;
- transitions;
- sound direction;
- final hero shot;
- product-fidelity constraints;
- prohibited alterations;
- overall rendering quality.

Do not produce shallow prompts such as:

“Create a cinematic premium advertisement of this product with dramatic lighting and camera movement.”

That is unacceptable.

The generated prompt must contain meaningful product-specific creative direction.

---

# PROMPT DEPTH

The default finished generation prompt should normally contain approximately:

**500–1,000 English words**

when the selected video model can benefit from detailed instructions.

Do not artificially shorten a strong prompt merely to satisfy a low word count.

However, detail must remain useful.

Do not inflate the prompt with repeated adjectives, duplicated prohibitions or meaningless cinematic terminology.

The goal is:

**high information density, not verbosity for its own sake.**

For generators that respond better to shorter instructions, the configured provider capability may define a shorter recommended range.

Provider-specific requirements override the default length.

---

# REQUIRED PROMPT ARCHITECTURE

When appropriate for the product and target model, structure the generated prompt conceptually around the following sections.

The exact headings do not have to appear every time, but the underlying information should be present.

## PRODUCT REFERENCE / SOURCE OF TRUTH

Clearly establish that the uploaded product reference is the authoritative visual source.

Example direction:

“Use the supplied product reference as the single visual source of truth.”

Specify the product characteristics that must remain consistent based on what is actually visible:

- silhouette;
- proportions;
- dimensions as visually established;
- color placement;
- visible materials;
- textures;
- surface finish;
- packaging;
- components;
- hardware;
- stitching;
- sole construction;
- closure;
- buttons;
- cap;
- labels;
- logos;
- typography placement;
- other established design details.

Never claim knowledge of a characteristic that cannot actually be established from the uploaded references.

Do not use generic fidelity lists mechanically.

Adapt the fidelity instructions to the actual product.

---

# PRODUCT LOCK

Include a strong product-preservation section.

The generated prompt should explicitly prevent relevant unwanted changes such as:

- redesigning the product;
- recoloring it;
- changing proportions;
- changing visible materials;
- altering packaging;
- replacing labels;
- moving logos;
- inventing typography;
- adding branding;
- removing established details;
- adding components;
- duplicating parts;
- changing closures;
- changing hardware;
- changing stitching;
- changing sole geometry;
- changing bottle or container shape.

Only include constraints relevant to the actual product.

Do not claim the video model is guaranteed to maintain perfect pixel-level consistency.

Use strong production instructions rather than false guarantees.

---

# CREATIVE DIRECTION

Every generated prompt must establish a clear advertising identity.

It should define the commercial using product-appropriate characteristics such as:

- premium;
- energetic;
- clean;
- minimal;
- fashionable;
- technical;
- youthful;
- refined;
- natural;
- bold;
- elegant;
- powerful;
- playful;
- authentic;
- cinematic.

Do NOT automatically describe every product as:

- luxury;
- premium;
- futuristic;
- high-energy.

Choose the direction from:

1. product category;
2. product appearance;
3. user-selected goal;
4. target audience;
5. selected style;
6. gallery inspiration when supplied.

The commercial should feel intentionally art-directed rather than randomly cinematic.

---

# COMMERCIAL IDEA

The prompt must have one clear advertising idea.

Examples:

- reveal craftsmanship;
- demonstrate tactile quality;
- turn product usage into the visual hook;
- showcase form through controlled light;
- build an energetic fashion reveal;
- make the packaging feel iconic;
- emphasize portability;
- show authentic everyday use;
- create a dramatic product launch;
- highlight one seller-confirmed functional benefit.

The commercial should not merely rotate the product in front of a camera unless that is intentionally the strongest concept.

---

# OPENING HOOK

The first visual moment must deliberately capture attention.

The prompt should describe an actual hook rather than writing:

“Start with an attention-grabbing shot.”

Examples may include:

- an extreme detail resolving into the complete product;
- controlled foreground wipe;
- rapid push through environmental elements;
- product emerging through moving light;
- tactile human interaction;
- unexpected framing;
- strong silhouette reveal;
- motion transition;
- macro texture reveal;
- visual action directly related to the product.

The hook must remain physically plausible and product-appropriate.

---

# CAMERA DIRECTION

Camera instructions must be deliberate and executable.

Possible techniques include:

- slow push-in;
- controlled dolly;
- lateral tracking;
- macro glide;
- orbit;
- half-orbit;
- crane movement;
- handheld drift;
- snap push;
- whip pan;
- crash zoom;
- pull-back reveal;
- low-angle hero tracking;
- overhead movement;
- parallax;
- focus pull;
- speed ramp;
- restrained roll;
- static hero hold.

Do not automatically use all of them.

Select only movements that support the concept.

High-energy commercials may use stronger camera movement.

Premium minimalist commercials may use slower, highly controlled camera motion.

UGC-style advertisements may use natural handheld behavior.

Fashion advertisements may use movement following talent or garment motion.

Product fidelity is more important than unnecessary camera complexity.

Avoid revealing unknown product surfaces merely to create an orbit.

---

# PRODUCT MOTION

Describe how the product behaves.

Product motion must respect:

- gravity;
- scale;
- construction;
- material;
- joints;
- flexibility;
- real-world use.

Possible motion may include:

- rotation;
- controlled spin;
- sliding;
- placement;
- unfolding when genuinely applicable;
- fabric motion;
- opening;
- closing;
- handling;
- walking;
- pouring;
- lifting;
- natural impact;
- camera-driven apparent motion.

Floating, levitation, self-assembly, explosion into components or impossible transformations should only be used when:

1. stylistically appropriate;
2. clearly intentional;
3. product integrity can still be maintained;
4. the target model can plausibly execute the effect.

Do not use unrealistic product animation simply because it looks dramatic.

---

# ENVIRONMENT

Specify an environment that strengthens the product identity.

Possible environments include:

- minimal studio;
- premium dark stage;
- bright seamless studio;
- real home;
- modern apartment;
- workspace;
- boutique;
- city environment;
- gym;
- outdoor location;
- clean architectural space;
- abstract geometry;
- reflective stage;
- textured editorial set;
- category-specific real-world environment.

Do not automatically place every product inside a dark luxury studio.

The environment must never overpower the product.

Describe only the necessary environmental details.

---

# LIGHTING

Lighting instructions must help reveal the actual product.

Possible techniques include:

- soft key light;
- directional key;
- edge lighting;
- rim light;
- moving highlight;
- controlled specular reflection;
- large softbox reflection;
- subtle bounce;
- backlight;
- narrow light sweep;
- soft shadows;
- high-key studio lighting;
- low-key cinematic lighting.

Lighting should support the real visible material.

For reflective products, control reflections.

For fabric, reveal texture and drape.

For matte packaging, avoid excessive glare.

For transparent containers, preserve believable transmission and refraction only when the actual product supports it.

Do not hide product color under extreme color grading.

---

# MATERIAL AND TEXTURE TREATMENT

Where useful, explicitly describe how important visible materials should react to:

- light;
- movement;
- contact;
- camera proximity.

For example:

- brushed metal;
- glossy plastic;
- matte paper;
- leather;
- suede;
- textile;
- glass;
- transparent plastic;
- polished ceramic;
- rubber;
- coated cardboard.

Only use material descriptions supported by the references or seller-confirmed information.

Do not convert an apparent material into an unsupported factual claim.

---

# TALENT AND HUMAN INTERACTION

When humans improve the commercial, describe:

- appropriate adult talent;
- visible body area;
- wardrobe;
- movement;
- handling;
- interaction;
- emotional tone.

Talent should complement the product.

Never let talent dominate unless the category requires it.

Adapt styling to the category.

Examples:

Formal shoe:
tailored or smart-casual styling.

Sports shoe:
athletic or active styling.

Premium garment:
editorial fashion styling.

Household product:
believable home context.

Electronics:
clean contemporary lifestyle or workspace.

Do not use one universal model or clothing template.

---

# CINEMATIC SEQUENCE

The prompt should describe an understandable visual progression.

For a supported short clip, structure the sequence according to available duration.

A possible 8-second commercial might use:

**0.0–1.5 s — Hook**

Attention-grabbing introduction.

**1.5–4.5 s — Product character / feature**

Primary product moment.

**4.5–6.5 s — Hero movement or interaction**

Strongest visual payoff.

**6.5–8.0 s — Hero ending**

Clean final product presentation.

This structure is flexible.

Do not force four scenes when two controlled beats would produce better video consistency.

The commercial should feel cohesive rather than like a demo reel of camera effects.

**the product must remain understandable.**

# HERO ENDING

Every commercial needs a deliberate ending.

Describe:

- product position;
- composition;
- camera behavior;
- light behavior;
- background;
- final hold.

The ending should make the product visually memorable.

A typical hero ending may use:

- centered product;
- slight low angle;
- controlled final push;
- clean light sweep;
- soft background separation;
- stable final hold.

If the user supplied a separate Brand Logo and requested an end card, it may appear here.

Do not place that separate logo onto the physical product.

Do not create additional branding.

---

# AUDIO

When the default silent-commercial workflow is selected, describe only realistic audio direction such as:

- fabric movement;
- footsteps;
- product handling;
- surface contact;
- bottle movement;
- mechanical click;
- subtle room ambience.

Music can be described only when the workflow supports or requests it.

Do not automatically request dialogue or narration.

If Uzbek voiceover is requested, preserve the exact Uzbek wording separately and only place it inside the video prompt when supported.

The finished prompt should contain an appropriate final rendering-quality direction.

Possible language may include:

- ultra-photorealistic;
- professional commercial cinematography;
- accurate material response;
- realistic reflections;
- physically believable lighting;
- detailed textures;
- natural depth of field;
- controlled cinematic contrast;

- zero artifacts;
- perfect geometry;
- perfect text reproduction;

Phrase them as strong output requirements rather than guaranteed outcomes.

# NEGATIVE / AVOIDANCE DIRECTION

Include a concise negative section tailored to the product.

Potential issues:

- no product redesign;
- no invented logos;
- no duplicated components;
- no morphing;
- no extra labels;
- no altered typography;
- no incorrect colors;
- no warped geometry;
- no incorrect anatomy;
- no unrealistic hand contact;
- no distracting props;
- no excessive particles;
- no unnecessary haze;
- no unrelated background objects.

Do not dump a universal hundred-item negative prompt into every generation.

---

# PRODUCT-SPECIFIC LANGUAGE

The final prompt must clearly demonstrate that the application understood the uploaded product.

Bad:

“Show the product in a premium studio.”

Better:

“Keep the dark green cylindrical bottle upright as the camera performs a restrained lateral macro glide across the existing front label, allowing the narrow highlight to trace the curved glossy surface without obscuring the printed branding.”

Specificity is a major differentiator of JAS Ad Studio.

The generated prompt must not look like something that could be reused unchanged for ten unrelated products.

---

# COMMERCIAL QUALITY BAR

Before accepting a prompt, internally ask:

1. Could this prompt have been written without seeing the product?

If yes, it is too generic.

2. Does the commercial have a clear visual idea?

If no, improve it.

3. Are camera directions actually executable?

If no, simplify them.

4. Does every major cinematic choice support the product?

If no, remove it.

5. Could the prompt accidentally alter the product?

If yes, strengthen product-reference instructions.

6. Does it contain unsupported claims?

If yes, remove them.

7. Is it detailed enough that a skilled commercial director could understand the intended advertisement?

If no, expand it.

8. Is it merely a long list of cinematic buzzwords?

If yes, rewrite it as a coherent commercial.
